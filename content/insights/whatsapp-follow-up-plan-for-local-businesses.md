Most local businesses work hard for the first visit and then leave the second one to chance. The customer liked the haircut, the meal, the saree or the consultation, went home, and was never heard from again. Not because they were unhappy, but because nobody gave them a reason to come back at the right moment.

The second visit is where a business is actually built. You have already paid to win that customer once: the rent on a good location, the ads, the signboard, the first impression. Bringing them back costs a message. And in Tamil Nadu, that message belongs on WhatsApp, the app your customers already open many times a day.

This guide gives you a plan you can start this week: how to get permission, which version of WhatsApp to use, the four messages that do most of the work, when to send them for your kind of business, and how to tell whether it is working.

## Before you send anything: get permission

This is the step most businesses skip, and it is the one that protects your number.

WhatsApp's [Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy) is clear: you may only contact someone on WhatsApp if they have given you their number **and** you have received their permission to message them. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act points the same way: consent should be clear, specific, and easy to withdraw. Beyond the rules, permission is simply good business. A customer who agreed to hear from you reads the message. A customer who didn't, blocks you. If enough people block or report a number, WhatsApp can restrict it.

Three easy ways to get permission:

- **At the counter or at billing.** "Can we send you a thank-you and the occasional update on WhatsApp?" Write "WA – yes" and the date against their name in your register or billing software.
- **A QR code at the counter.** It opens a chat with your business with a ready-typed message such as *"Hi, I'd like updates from [your business]."* The customer sends the first message, so the permission is theirs and on record.
- **A tick box on your booking or enquiry form.** Leave it unticked. Only message people who tick it.

> **Rule of thumb:** if you can't point to when and how a customer said yes, don't message them.

## The app or the platform: which one you need

WhatsApp offers two ways for a business to message customers. Most local businesses should start with the first.

| | WhatsApp Business app | WhatsApp Business Platform (API) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Free | Charged per template message, at rates set by Meta by category and country |
| **Good for** | A few hundred customers, one or two people replying | Larger lists, several staff on one number, automation |
| **Starting a conversation** | Broadcast lists, which only reach people who have saved your number | Pre-approved message templates, sent to anyone who has opted in |
| **Automation** | Greeting message, away message, quick replies, labels | Reminders triggered by your billing, booking or CRM software |
| **Set-up** | Download and verify your number | Through Meta or a WhatsApp solution provider, with templates approved in advance |

Two details matter on the platform. Since July 2025, Meta charges for each template message rather than for each conversation, with different rates for marketing, utility and authentication messages ([current pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). And once a customer messages you, a 24-hour customer service window opens, in which you can reply freely; outside that window, a business can only start the conversation with an approved template.

Start on the app. Move to the platform when you can no longer keep up by hand, or when you want reminders to go out on their own.

## The four messages that bring customers back

You do not need a newsletter, a loyalty app or a daily offer. Four well-timed messages do most of the work. Each one has a single job.

### 1. The thank-you (same day)

**Its job:** make the visit feel noticed, and open the door for any problem to come to you, not to a review site.

> **English:** Hi [Name], thank you for visiting [Your business] today! If anything isn't right, just reply here and we'll sort it out. Reply STOP if you'd rather not get messages from us.
>
> **தமிழ்:** வணக்கம் [பெயர்], இன்று [உங்கள் கடை]க்கு வந்ததற்கு நன்றி! ஏதாவது சரியில்லை என்றால் இங்கேயே பதில் அனுப்புங்கள், உடனே சரி செய்கிறோம். எங்களிடமிருந்து செய்திகள் வேண்டாம் என்றால் STOP என்று அனுப்புங்கள்.

### 2. The check-in (once the product or service is in use)

**Its job:** show you care about the result, not only the sale. This is the message people remember.

> **English:** Hi [Name], it's been a week since your visit. How is the [product/service] working out? If anything needs a small fix, we're happy to help.
>
> **தமிழ்:** வணக்கம் [பெயர்], நீங்கள் வந்து ஒரு வாரம் ஆகிறது. [பொருள்/சேவை] எப்படி இருக்கிறது? சிறிய மாற்றம் ஏதாவது தேவைப்பட்டால், மகிழ்ச்சியுடன் உதவுகிறோம்.

### 3. The reason to return (just before they would naturally come back)

**Its job:** make the next visit easy. Not a discount, a reminder with a clear next step.

> **English:** Hi [Name], it's nearly time for your next [haircut / check-up / refill]. Shall we keep a slot for you this Saturday? Reply YES and we'll confirm.
>
> **தமிழ்:** வணக்கம் [பெயர்], உங்கள் அடுத்த [ஹேர்கட் / செக்-அப் / ரீஃபில்] நேரம் நெருங்கிவிட்டது. இந்த சனிக்கிழமை உங்களுக்காக ஒரு நேரம் ஒதுக்கட்டுமா? YES என்று பதில் அனுப்புங்கள்.

### 4. The win-back (when they have been away too long)

**Its job:** find out why, and invite them back with something real.

> **English:** Hi [Name], we haven't seen you in a while and wanted to check everything is okay. If there's anything we could have done better, we'd really like to hear it. And if you'd like to come back, [a genuine reason: a new arrival, a seasonal service] is waiting for you.
>
> **தமிழ்:** வணக்கம் [பெயர்], உங்களை நீண்ட நாட்களாகப் பார்க்கவில்லை. எங்கள் சேவையில் ஏதாவது குறை இருந்திருந்தால் தயவுசெய்து சொல்லுங்கள். மீண்டும் வர விரும்பினால், [புதிதாக வந்தது / சீசன் சேவை] உங்களுக்காகக் காத்திருக்கிறது.

Write your Tamil messages in Tamil from the start, the way you would say them at the counter. A message translated word for word from English reads like a bank SMS, and gets treated like one.

## When to send them: timing by business

The right timing depends on how often your customers naturally come back. A salon customer returns in weeks; a saree customer, from festival to festival. Base your timing on that cycle, not on a fixed "monthly newsletter" calendar.

The table below gives starting points. Your own billing records will tell you the real gap between visits; adjust once you know it.

| Business | Usual gap between visits | Check-in | Reason to return | Win-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salon or spa | 4–6 weeks | Day 3 | Week 4–5 | Week 10 |
| Clinic or dental practice | As the doctor advises | Day 2, on recovery | Just before the review is due | 2 weeks after a missed review |
| Restaurant or café | 2–4 weeks | Not needed; the thank-you is enough | Week 2–3, with a new dish or weekend special | Week 8 |
| Clothing or saree showroom | Festival to festival | Day 7, on fit and alterations | 2–3 weeks before the next festival | After that festival has passed |
| Pharmacy or grocery | Each refill or month | Not needed | 3 days before a refill is due | 1 week after a missed refill |
| Coaching or tuition | Term to term | Week 1, on settling in | Before the next term's admissions | Once admissions open |

For clinics and anything health-related, keep messages general. Never put a diagnosis, test result or medicine name in a WhatsApp message.

## What not to do

- **Don't buy number lists** or message people who never gave you their number. It breaks WhatsApp's policy and gets numbers restricted.
- **Don't add customers to a WhatsApp group** without asking. A group shows every member's number to everyone else in it.
- **Don't message every day.** If a message wouldn't be useful to that customer at that moment, don't send it.
- **Don't send everyone the same offer.** A reminder for a refill means nothing to someone who came in for a gift.
- **Don't make it hard to stop.** Every first message should tell people how to opt out, and you should honour it the same day.

## How to know it's working

Messages read and replies received are useful signals, but they are not the goal. The goal is customers who come back. Track one number every month:

> **Repeat rate** = customers who bought **two or more times** in the last 90 days ÷ customers who bought **at least once** in the same 90 days

Work it out from your billing software, or from a simple sheet with each customer's name, number, opt-in date, first visit, last visit and number of visits. Note your repeat rate before you start, then watch how it moves over the next three months. If it rises, your follow-ups are doing their job. If it doesn't, look at the timing first, then the message.

## Your first 30 days

1. **Week 1:** Start asking for WhatsApp permission at every bill. Put a QR code on the counter.
2. **Week 1:** Set up the WhatsApp Business app with your business name, address, hours, a greeting message and quick replies for the thank-you and check-in.
3. **Week 2:** Look at your records and write down the usual gap between visits for your main customer types.
4. **Week 2:** Write your four messages in Tamil and English and save them as quick replies.
5. **Weeks 2–4:** Send the thank-you every day, and the check-ins as they fall due.
6. **Day 30:** Work out your repeat rate. Decide whether you need automation (the WhatsApp Business Platform) or whether the app is still enough.
